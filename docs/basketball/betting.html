Model versus market

The model does not beat the market, and this page is not a card to bet. It is published because the comparison is interesting and because a system that only shows you its good weeks is not telling you anything.

Two habits, tracked

Two ways these games have been bet for years: taking the underdog when the model has them winning outright, and betting against teams the market keeps getting wrong. Both are tracked here from the first game of the season. Tracked, not recommended.

A person decides, not the list. Every pick below comes from a fixed rule applied to the model and the market, and nothing more. It is a starting point for human judgement, never a bet on its own: before any money goes down, someone reads the game — injuries, lineups, travel, motivation, whatever the numbers cannot see — and makes the call. The record logs every pick the rule makes, whether or not anyone bet it, so it measures the rule by itself rather than those decisions.

What the backtest says

Model picks the underdog to win51.0% ATS 907–873 over 4 seasons (ESPN Bet), -48.5 units. 49.9% on DraftKings last season (230–231). Bet the underdog with the points.
Fade teams that keep failing to cover48.2% ATS 425–456 over 4 seasons (ESPN Bet), -69.6 units. 49.1% on DraftKings last season (230–238). They tend to start covering: the market has already adjusted.

Break-even at −110 is 52.38%. Graded on the model as it stood the morning of each game, and each team’s cover record from earlier games only.

One corner looks alive: underdog picks getting 10 or more points went 21–6. Almost all are November games where the model barely knows one team, the same failure the disagreement table below describes, and it did not hold on DraftKings. Worth watching, not betting.

One bucket clears significance: gaps of 15 points or more went 54–25. There are 79 of those in 16,954 games, 42 of them in the first season tested, and reading them is the end of the story — they are November mismatches against opponents the model has barely seen. In one, the model favoured Mississippi Valley State by four where the market had Hawai'i by 25.5. It won the bet while being wrong by 22 points. The bucket is measuring the model's own failures, and it does not reappear on DraftKings.

The one faint signal

Betting the opening number rather than the close, mean closing line value is positive and grows with the size of the disagreement — the market tends to drift toward this model's side after the opener. That is a reason to keep measuring, not a reason to bet, and it is the same shape of hint football produced.
